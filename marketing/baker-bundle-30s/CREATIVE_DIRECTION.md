# The Baker: "Better Together" 30 s bundle film
**The Baker 100L Oven + Artisan SE 10L Stand Mixer · The Baker Official Store, Shopee Mall**

Master format: **9:16, 1080×1920, 30 fps**. Cut-downs are covered in section D.
Tone: premium, warm and unhurried, like KitchenAid or Apple ecosystem ads. It is not a flash-sale ad.
Positioning: a complete home-baking set. There are no business, income or commercial angles anywhere in the film.

**What the assets imply.** They are all clean studio packshots on transparent backgrounds. There is no food, no people and no live-action footage. So this film is a **product-led motion film**, and three things carry the emotion instead of footage:

1. **Light.** The frame gets warmer and brighter as the story moves forward, and the oven window glows once it starts baking.
2. **Sound.** Food and family sounds carry what the camera doesn't show: dough, crackle, the timer bell, laughter.
3. **Words.** A few lines of VO and minimal supers.

Two optional stock overlays help and add no new product visuals: a **flour puff** in S2 and **heat shimmer** in S6. Both are standard licensed overlays, and the film works without them.

### Answers to your five questions

1. **Strongest first impression.** The **oven window glowing amber** (`_OEK6146.png` or `_OEK6149.png` plus the existing element-glow effect). Warm light behind glass is the closest thing in the assets to "something delicious is baking". It stops the scroll without looking like an ad. A cold packshot in the first frame reads as "ad" and gets skipped. Runner-up: a macro of the **red enamel** (`SM-A02 RED 1.png`), because saturated red is the strongest colour contrast in a Shopee feed. It's used as Hook B in section H.
2. **Best link between mixer and oven.**
   * **S5:** one continuous camera move from the mixer to the oven, in the same room and under the same light.
   * **S7:** a circle-to-circle match cut from the mixer's speed dial (`SM-A02 RED 1.png`) to the oven's convection fan (`_OEK6146.png`). The shared shape and position say "designed as one system" without a word.
   * **S9:** the two products facing each other in one composition.
3. **Emotional momentum.** Three things build together:
   * **Framing** goes from macro to medium to wide. The frame opens as the story opens.
   * **Light** goes from soft morning cream to amber oven glow to golden.
   * **Music** layers in one instrument group every few bars.
   Nothing gets faster. It only gets fuller.
4. **Perceived bundle value.**
   * Tell one story with two halves, never two product demos.
   * Show everything in the set: the oven, the mixer and all three attachments. More items read as more value.
   * Once the products meet (S8 to S10), never show either one alone again.
   * Call it "the complete baking set", not "bundle deal".
   * Price, if you use it, comes last and small, as confirmation rather than the reason.
5. **A satisfying ending.** The timer bell rings, the warm family sounds come in, and the light blooms to golden. The pair holds on screen and the line lands: "Better together." The music resolves on that word. The last frame holds still, and its glowing oven window matches the first frame, so the film loops cleanly on TikTok and Shopee Video.

### The big idea: "The Handoff"
The mixer's story ends at dough and the oven's story starts at dough. **Neither half is complete on its own.** The film passes the bake forward: mixer, then oven, then family. The viewer is never told to buy both. They just watch one product finish what the other started, and they conclude it themselves.

## D. Editing instructions

**Pacing**
* 10 shots in 30 s, average 3 s. Every cut lands on a bar downbeat at 84 BPM. If your track has a different tempo, keep the shot order and snap each cut to its nearest downbeat.
* Only two cuts land off the bar: the S7 match cut (half bar) and the bell cut into S8.
* S8 to S10 is **one continuous move** (8.6 s). The emotional peak gets the longest unbroken take.

**Motion rules**
* **Push-ins and pull-outs:** 3–6% scale per shot with a cubic ease in and out. Never more than 8%. No punch zooms.
* **2.5D parallax:** separate product, counter and wall. The background moves at 30–40% of the product speed.
* **Light sweeps:** a soft specular band at about 20°, taking 0.9 s. Use it once in S2, once in S3 and once in S9, where **one sweep crosses both products**. The shared light makes them read as one set.
* **Rotations (S3, S6, S7):** mask the dial, knob or fan from the still and rotate it. Add 2–3 frames of motion blur. Rotation speed and direction must match exactly across the S7 cut.
* **Resolution limits:** the mixer and attachment photos can take heavy crops. The oven fan and knob crops from `_OEK6146.png` and `_OEK6149.png` are near their limit, so keep the S7 circle at about 300–350 px on screen and let the glow and slight blur hide the softness.

**Transitions.** There are only three kinds:
1. Hard cuts on the beat (most of them).
2. One light dissolve, glow to glint, in S1 to S2.
3. One match cut, dial to fan, in S7.

No whip pans, glitches, zoom blurs or shape wipes.

**Text animation:** fade plus a 12 px rise over 10–12 frames in, and a fade over 8 frames out. No slams, bounces or typewriter effects. The headline fades out before the next one fades in, so only one headline is ever on screen.

**Look**
* **World:** a warm cream wall (#F6F0E8 to #E9DFD2 gradient), a light oak or stone counter (about #D9C6AE), and soft window light from the upper left.
* **Packshot relighting:** the packshots were shot on cool gray, so warm them up about 8 points on temperature. Add a soft contact shadow and a 12–15% floor reflection, and light them from the upper left to match the room.
* **Light arc:** S1–S5 is soft morning, S6–S7 is amber from the oven, S8–S10 blooms to golden, about +20% exposure by the end.
* **Oven glow:** reuse the element-glow and knob-turn code in `marketing/baker-promo-oct-2026/index.html`, recoloured from red-orange to warm amber (about #FFB45A).

**Deliverables**
* **9:16 master:** 1080×1920, 30 fps, H.264 at about 16 Mbps, AAC 320 kbps. Audio at −14 LUFS integrated, true peak −1 dBTP. For Shopee Video, TikTok, and Reels or Stories.
* **1:1 (1080×1080) and 4:5 (1080×1350) reframes:** the pair shots suit square well. For the Shopee listing video and Meta feed. Check Shopee Seller Centre's current length and file-size limits before upload.
* **Captioned 4:5 variant for Meta feed:** burn in VO captions there, where most people watch with sound off. The 9:16 master stays clean.
* **15 s cut-down:** S1 (2.9 s), S2 (2.9 s), S5 (2.9 s), S7 (2.8 s), then S9 and S10 merged (3.5 s). VO: lines 1 and 2, "The mixer starts it. The oven finishes it.", then "Better together. On Shopee Mall."

## G. Psychology breakdown

| Scene | Principle | What it does toward "getting both just makes sense" |
|---|---|---|
| S1 Glow | **Reward preview** and curiosity | The first frame promises the outcome (warmth, baking) before any selling. "A great bake starts…" opens a question the viewer wants closed |
| S2 Red mixer | **Reframing** | Most viewers arrive thinking "oven". "…before the oven" moves where the bake begins to the mixer, so the mixer becomes part of what they wanted all along |
| S3 Mix with confidence | **Mental simulation** and ownership | The slow reveal and turning dial let the viewer imagine using it. That's the early "endowment" feeling of already owning it |
| S4 Whisk · Beat · Knead | **Completeness** and **upgrade psychology** | Three tools on three beats show breadth. "The whole batch, in one go" names a frustration of smaller equipment (mixing in rounds) without showing a competitor |
| S5 Handoff | **Continuity (Gestalt)** and **complementary goods** | One continuous move puts both products in one space. The mixer's output is literally the oven's input, so each is worth more with the other |
| S6 Bake with confidence | **Capacity**, less waiting, **savouring** | "Room for every tray" means one bake, not three. The silence lets anticipation build, and the premium pace signals quality |
| S7 Match cut | **Visual rhyme = designed as a system** | The same circle, size and spin across both products reads as shared design DNA, the Apple and Dyson ecosystem technique. "The mixer starts it. The oven finishes it." states the dependency: each is half a story |
| S8 Bell + family | **Benefit ladder** (feature → function → emotion) | The bell resolves the curiosity from S1. Family sounds move the benefit from "a better appliance" to "more time sharing". For Malaysian home bakers, baking is about sharing: open houses, festive tins, family weekends |
| S9 The pair | **Unit perception** and the **Diderot effect** | Products that share a light, a shadow and a sweep read as one set. Once they've been seen as a pair, taking one away feels incomplete. That's the "if I'm already upgrading, I might as well complete it" instinct |
| S10 Better together | **Closure**, double meaning, **emotion first and logic second** | "Together" now means both the family and the products. Price comes last and small, as rational confirmation for a feeling already formed. The Shopee Mall badge and Official Store name reduce risk (authenticity, warranty) |

**Why nothing is said outright.** The film never says "buy both" or "why buy separately?". The structure makes that argument: the mixer's half ends unfinished, the oven's half starts from someone else's dough, and neither product appears alone again after S7. A conclusion the viewer reaches themselves persuades far more than one they're told. For an A/B test, a more explicit end line is: VO *"Why get one, when they work as one?"* in place of "The Baker Oven and Artisan SE Mixer."
